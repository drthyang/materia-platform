# Can lines: is an extra peak the fit does not capture a can line?

The owner's request: "We are doing neutron diffraction, add the feature that I
can check if the diffraction signals overlap to Vanadium or Aluminum can
background." On review the owner set the question: "The can lines should be
like some extra peaks not captured by refinement, and we can have a
convenient way to check, I don't want to make it too overwhelming." The
powder page answers it. Neutron data only (constant wavelength and time of
flight). Nothing is fitted or excluded. Nothing of it reaches the Copilot
(no tool, no `get_state` field, no model-visible text).

## What the page shows

- **A "Can lines" toggle** in the plot's toolbar, beside the phase ticks. Two
  tick rows join the phase rows: **V can** and **Al can**. Each tick has two
  parts. A faint full-height mark shows where the line is. A solid bar rises
  from the row's foot to the line's true relative strength per volume of
  metal, so the vanadium lines are drawn as weak as they are. V's strongest
  line is 1/20 to 1/40 of Al's: 1/43 at λ 1.5 Å, 1/19 at 2.41 Å, 1/23 on a
  difC 9000 bank. With the toggle on, each extra peak that lines up with a
  can line is **labelled on the difference curve** ("Al 220"). On X-ray data
  the toggle is absent.
- **A "Can lines" card in Validation, result first**:
  - The first line is "1 extra peak lines up with a can line: 1.432 Å (Al 220)
    view →", or "No extra peak lines up with a V or Al can line."
  - Then "1 other extra peak matches no can line: 3.882 Å view →". The first
    five are listed; any more sit behind "+ N more".
  - Each can's d-scale sits small under it. Beside a scale other than 1, it
    shows the shift that scale makes at the can's strongest line, in FWHM.
  - When the data file states the sample temperature, a button offers the
    cooled value from the tabulated thermal expansion, with its shift. For a
    stated 100 K on NOMAD bank 4: V × 0.99864 (211, −0.2 FWHM) and Al × 0.99629
    (311, −0.6 FWHM).
  - No temperature is assumed. A number without a unit does not count, nor
    does a sample name (`…_at_300K`), nor a value at or below 0 K (the NOMAD
    reduction's placeholder "at temperature 0.0 K"). Two different
    temperatures in one file give none.
  - Last comes one collapsed line: "Al and V lines also sit under 91 sample
    reflections: their intensities may include the can." Behind its **show**
    is the list of those overlaps, strongest can line first. Each row gives
    the can line, its relative intensity, the sample reflection (reflections
    of one phase at one d are one row, e.g. 822/660), the separation in FWHM
    and a plain reading. No table is open by default.

## The extra-peak rule

The card adds no detector. Its extra peaks are those the Validation view
already shows in "What limits the fit", in its "Lines no phase explains"
row. They are the Copilot's `diagnose_fit` `unexplainedExcess`
(`backgroundTerms.ts:unexplainedExcess`, read on the page through
`pageFitDiagnosis`). On the same data the card, the view and the Copilot's
`diagnose_fit` therefore name the same peaks. The card's "?" states the rule:

> An extra peak is intensity the model does not capture, as Validation's
> "What limits the fit" (and the Copilot's diagnose_fit) finds it.
>
> - It is looked for only on the points where the model is background alone
>   (its peaks under 0.5 σ there).
> - The residual (obs − calc)/σ, with its running mean over 24 line widths
>   taken off, is summed over windows of 1, 2, 4 and 8 line widths (the FWHM
>   of the calculated lines).
> - A window counts when that sum over √(points) passes the level that pure
>   noise crosses anywhere in the pattern only 1 time in 100 (about 4σ on a
>   few thousand points). The peak is placed at its strongest window.
> - σ is the file's own, or √y where it gives none.
>
> A peak lines up with a can line when it is within 1 FWHM of the page's
> profile at the can line's d, after that can's d-scale. Peaks under the
> sample's own lines are not looked for here: the can lines under sample
> reflections are listed below.

Other detectors in the code read the residual differently. The Copilot's
`find_unexplained_peaks` is one; it has no width requirement and a 5σ
per-point threshold. A local note compares them, with examples where they
disagree. Choosing one rule for all of them would change model-visible
output, so that is left to the owner. The hub's "3σ over about one FWHM" idea
is offered there as a candidate to replace the shown detector, not as a
further one.

## How each decision is implemented

- **Data-driven cans** (`src/core/diagnostics/canLines.ts`, `CAN_PHASES`):
  each entry names a phase of the known-phase library
  (`knownPhases.ts`: Al Fm-3m a 4.0495 Å, V Im-3m a 3.0240 Å, room
  temperature) and optionally an expansion table. Cu, steel or Nb is one more
  entry (Cu, α-Fe, austenitic steel and Nb are already in the library); the
  plot colour falls back to a neutral one for a can without its own.
- **(a) Lattice.** d = d₂₉₃ × s per can, s stated and adjustable. The cooled
  value is a(T)/a(293 K) = 1 + ΔL/L₀(T), interpolated in the cited table as a
  cubic Hermite with α as the slope, the slopes limited (Fritsch–Carlson) so
  the curve never turns back between rows; no value outside the table's
  range. The library cells are taken as 293 K values (the 5 K to 298 K moves
  Al's d by 1·10⁻⁴).
- **The expansion data**: Y. S. Touloukian, R. K. Kirby, R. E. Taylor and
  P. D. Desai, *Thermophysical Properties of Matter*, Vol. 12: *Thermal
  Expansion, Metallic Elements and Alloys* (IFI/Plenum, New York, 1975),
  recommended values, Table 1R (Al, 5–900 K, ±3 %) and Table 60R (V,
  5–2000 K; ±10 % below 293 K, provisional there; ±5 % above), copied from
  the printed tables (DTIC AD-A129115). Al cross-checked against
  Corruccini and Gniewek, *Thermal Expansion of Technical Solids at Low
  Temperatures*, NBS Monograph 29 (1961), Table 2.1.
- **(b) Match** = within one FWHM of the page's own profile at the can
  line's d (an extra peak, or a sample reflection for the collapsed list). The profile is the page's isotropic one (instrument terms and the
  isotropic sample terms, `placePeaks` without hkl), constant wavelength or
  time of flight; its apex and FWHM are measured on the shape as drawn (the
  apex where the slope changes sign, each half-maximum crossing by
  bisection), so FCJ asymmetry and back-to-back exponentials are measured, not
  approximated. Sample reflections are placed the same way; an extra peak at
  the detector's strongest window. The separation |x − x_can| / FWHM_can is printed to 0.01, and one printed
  1.00 counts as within.
- **(c) UI** (`src/app/ui/CanLines.tsx`):
  - The toggle sits in the plot toolbar.
  - Tick heights come from an optional `height` on a reflection tick. The
    plot draws it only when it is set, so every other tick row is drawn as
    before.
  - The labels on the difference curve come from an optional `diffMarks` on
    the plot.
  - The scale row sits under the plot and in the card. The card comes after
    the Validation view.
  - The plot receives the can rows and labels only while the toggle is on.
    The report's and the Agent's tick lists are untouched.
- **Intensities**: the can phase's |F|² with the engine's neutron coherent
  scattering lengths (V −0.3824 fm, Al 3.449 fm), its multiplicity, the
  page's Lorentz factor (on or off as the page) and intensity corrections
  (`placePeaks`). Relative within each can; across cans per unit volume
  (÷ V_cell²) for the tick heights. The section says the can's scale against
  the sample is unknown.

## Saved with the project

The toggle and the d-scales are an optional powder workspace field,
`canLines: {on, scales?}` (docs/PROJECT_FORMAT.md): written only when the view
is not at its defaults, scales of 1 left out, validated on read (a boolean
toggle, positive scales). They are restored with the other view settings on
Open and when going back in History. Like the axis unit they are display
only: a change is neither a History step nor a settings change in a step's
label. No schema version bump; a file written before the field opens with the
toggle off and every scale 1. Tests: save → reopen through the serializer and
validator keeps both; the fixture without the field opens with the defaults; a
malformed field is refused by name; a History record differing only in it is
no step. A browser round trip through Save and Open kept the toggle on and
Al × 0.99629.

  `src/app/canLinesExtraPeaks.test.ts` (7, about 1 s).
- Positions against an independent calculation written in the test
  (d = a/√(h²+k²+l²), fcc all-odd or all-even, bcc h+k+l even): every V and
  Al line and only those, at scales 1 and 0.9958, on two instruments.

    Gaussian's apex is there to 10⁻⁸°.
  - Time of flight (difC 5000): the ticks sit at difC·d, and the apex is

- The cooled d-scale matches the cited rows exactly (Al 50, 100, 200, 293 and
  500 K; V 25, 100 and 200 K). It is constant below 5 K and has no value past
  the table. Between rows it is within 4·10⁻⁵ of NBS Monograph 29 at 90, 140,
  180, 240 and 273 K, and it is monotonic. The shift in FWHM is checked
  against 2θ computed in the test.
- V against Al: per atom, I/(m·d⁴·DW·n²) is one number within each can, and

  `_at_300K` name are not taken. Two temperatures give none.

every existing tick row as before.

## Limits

- The can's own line widths are taken as the page's profile: a can's
  microstructure differs from the sample's.
- The extra peaks are the Validation view's own, with its limits. Nothing is
  looked for under the sample's lines (the collapsed list covers those). The
  detector needs three resolved lines to measure a width, and a background
  curve. It runs about 1 s on a 3000-point TOF bank each time the curves
  change while the toggle is on or Validation is open.
- A part away from the sample position shifts its lines by more than the
  cooled scale; the scale is the user's to set.
- Only the stated temperature is read from the file; a temperature in a
  separate log or a sample name is not.
